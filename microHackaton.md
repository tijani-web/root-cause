Overview
Build at the frontier of agentic AI. August 28–31, 2026. Online, individual, free.
AI can produce convincing code in seconds. Real engineering begins when convincing is not enough: incomplete requirements, hidden dependencies, difficult edge cases, failure modes, and decisions that require technical judgment.

The micro1 Frontier Engineering Challenge is a free, global, online competition for engineers ready to work at that frontier. Over a three-day sprint you will use coding agents to tackle a real-world software engineering problem and produce a solution that is correct, reproducible, testable and clearly explained.

 





 

The full challenge statement is released at kickoff (Aug 28 at 15:00 UTC). Until then, expect to build at the frontier of agentic AI without details that would reveal the problem in advance.

Participants will be able to registre after the Hackathon kicks off. The Hackathon ends on Aug 31 at 18:00 UTC.

Participants are evaluated on the quality of the submitted work, not résumé prestige, job title, university, employer or geography.

Coding-agent use is required. You must disclose the tools you used and submit the required trajectories for evaluation.

Every valid entry must present both a baseline solution and an advanced solution. The advanced solution should show a meaningful improvement in capability, reliability, efficiency, coverage or engineering quality, not a cosmetic variation.

 

Technology policy
Supported languages include Python, TypeScript, Java, C++, Go and Rust. Commonly used frameworks and libraries in those ecosystems are allowed, provided the entry stays reproducible and complies with the final problem PDF.

Illustrative examples: FastAPI, Flask, Django, LangGraph and related Python ecosystems; Node.js, Express, NestJS and Next.js for TypeScript; Spring Boot for Java; standard C++ and CMake toolchains; Go modules and common Go web frameworks; and Cargo, Tokio, Axum or Actix for Rust. This list is intentionally non-exhaustive.

The problem PDF may prescribe a starter repository, runtime, dependency limits, API access or testing environment where needed for fair and deterministic judging.

Eligibility Criteria
Participants must be at least 18 years old at the time of registration.
Open globally except where participation or payment is prohibited by applicable law, sanctions, export controls, or organizer and platform restrictions.
Entries are individual only. A participant may register once and submit one final entry. Revisions are allowed until the deadline; only the latest complete submission is evaluated.
Participants should have at least six months of practical software-building experience or equivalent hands-on evidence. Professional employment is not required.
Eligible participants include working engineers, founders, open-source contributors, competitive programmers, final-year students, graduate students and recent graduates who meet the experience standard.
micro1 employees, event administrators, judges, anyone involved in creating or testing the challenge, and their immediate household members are not eligible for prizes.
Participants must be able to receive payment through the approved payout rail in their country to receive a cash prize or trace-acquisition payment.
Accurate identity, location, contact and eligibility information is required. Duplicate or false registrations may be disqualified.

Submission Package


Complete solution code and improvement changelog
Share the full project and everything required to run it, including the instructions that shape each agent. Use the README to introduce the intended user, explain their current bottleneck, and describe why solving it is valuable. Add a clearly labelled Improvement Changelog with an entry for every meaningful iteration, connected to the evidence that guided your next decision. Close with the main failure mode and your hot take.

Reproduction guide. 
Written for someone starting from a clean environment. Walk them through setup and give the exact commands for the solution, the baseline and the evaluation. Explain which data is required and what output to expect. Share relevant versions along with approximate runtime and cost.

Solution video, up to 5 minutes. 
Begin with the problem and the simple baseline, then walk through one realistic execution from start to finish. Show the final comparison and briefly explain the changelog. Highlight the change that contributed most, and one experiment you removed.

Agent trajectories. 
Representative trajectories for every agent you used, easy to follow from the agent instructions through to the final result. Show what the agent did and how its tools responded. Capture the feedback that shaped its next step, plus any retries or human checkpoints.

Rule Book
You are welcome to build with tools and components you already know.

Make it clear what existed before the competition and what you added.

Use every tool and component according to its licence and service terms.

Keep consequential actions controlled through a sandbox or simulation. Add human approval before the action happens.

Make a qualified human reviewer part of any solution that could significantly affect someone.

Choose a legal and ethical use case that treats people and their data responsibly.

Use information you are allowed to share. Public or synthetic data are usually easiest. Approved anonymous data also works.

Keep credentials and private information outside the submission.

Connect every claim about your results to the evidence you submit.

Give judges enough access to run the project and reproduce the main result.

Submissions are governed by the Hackathon Participation Agreement (accepted at registration), under which micro1 owns submissions and may use them for AI model training and evaluation.

FAQs
Q: Is registration free?

A: Yes. There is no fee to register or submit.

 

Q: Can I participate as a team?

A: No. The August 2026 edition is for individual participation and individual prizes.

 

Q: When will the exact problem be available?

A: The exact problem will be revealed at kickoff on August 28th. Before then, micro1 will share the theme, rules, submission requirements, and schedule.

 

Q: Are AI tools allowed?

A: Yes. Coding-agent use is required. You must disclose the tools you use and submit the required trajectories or traces.

 

Q: Do you provide API keys or model credits?

A: No. Participants must use their own agent setup.

 

Q: Which programming languages can I use?

A: Any language is allowed, with Python, TypeScript, Java, C++, Go, and Rust recommended, subject to the runtime constraints in the final problem PDF.

 

Q: Do I need to be looking for a job to participate?

A: No. This is a competition. Any future interest in paid micro1 work will be handled separately.

 

Q: What makes a submission valid?

A: A valid submission must be timely, complete, original, policy compliant, and reproducible, and include the required repository, archive, tests, README, agent-use evidence, and demo video.

 

Q: Does every submission receive a certificate?

A: Yes. Every eligible participant with a valid submission receives a digital participation certificate.

 

Q: Does submitting guarantee trace reimbursement or a job?

A: No. Trace acquisition is a separate, limited programme if approved. Employment and project opportunities are separate and subject to additional review.

 

Q: How much time will this take?

A: Plan for a focused three-day weekend. Keep your problem scope tight so the deliverables are achievable within the timeframe.

 

Q: Where can I get help?

A: Use the organizer contact and Help options on the challenge page. Official clarifications will be shared with all participants.
